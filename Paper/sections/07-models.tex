\section{The Model May Not Speak Spanish}
\label{sec:model-may-not-speak-spanish}

This section answers the question the whole design exists for: \textbf{the operator
can bind any model, including one with no useful Spanish at all. How is correctness
still guaranteed?}

\subsection{A taxonomy of model deficiency}
\label{sec:taxonomy-of-model-deficiency}

``Not Spanish-capable'' is not one condition. It is four, and they fail in different
places.

\small

\label{tab:deficiency-taxonomy}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.16\textwidth}p{0.45\textwidth}p{0.27\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Class} & \textbf{Description} & \textbf{Where it shows up} \\
\midrule
\textbf{C1 --- Weak generation} &
Understands Spanish; writes it awkwardly or drifts into English mid-answer &

\addlinespace
\textbf{C2 --- Weak comprehension} &

\addlinespace

C3 and C4 are the dangerous ones precisely because they are \emph{silent}: the action
looks well-formed and executes successfully against the wrong target.

Note what is \emph{not} in the recovery column: nowhere does the system rely on the
model becoming better at Spanish. \textbf{Spanish competence is an accelerant, not a
dependency.} A model that has none simply spends more rungs, and the operator notices
only a slightly slower answer.

\subsection{The capability profile}
\label{sec:the-capability-profile}

Corollary 1 makes the profile safe; \S5.9 makes it necessary. Folding and
boundary-matching are free and unconditional, but \textbf{canonical-key
expansion is not free in the sense that matters}: appending English hint tokens
to a Spanish request raises the correct tool's score for a model whose Spanish
is thin, and \emph{lowers signal-to-noise} for a model that already understood
the sentence. Both directions are correctness failures. Something must decide,
per model, which of those two mistakes to risk --- and that is the entire job of
the capability profile.

The obvious design is a lookup. It is impossible, and establishing that is the
premise of everything else in this section.

\textbf{There is nothing to look up.}

\begin{keyinsight}
\textbf{Observation 1 (No Language Oracle).} No major model provider exposes
supported languages programmatically. Verified against primary sources,
2026-07-28 --- see Table~\ref{tab:no-language-oracle}.
\end{keyinsight}

The pattern is not an oversight of one vendor. Capability enums are real,
actively maintained and genuinely useful --- Ollama's eight values are
load-bearing elsewhere in this system --- and language is simply not among the
things a provider is willing to assert about a model. It is the one capability
nobody will certify.

The consequence is structural, and it is worth stating as a constraint rather
than a preference:

\begin{keyinsight}
No lookup can replace a hardcoded model-name table. The replacement must be a
measurement.
\end{keyinsight}

This is the finding that disposes of the design an earlier revision of this work
actually shipped: a regex over the model id deciding the tier outright. That is
the hardcoded table wearing a different hat. It survives here only in the
demoted role described below, hedged by three invariants, because the evidence
forbids it any authority.

\textbf{The one declaration that exists is worse than nothing.}

There is a single structured language field anywhere in the stack: the GGUF
metadata key \code{general.languages}. It is optional and author-supplied, and
measured across fifteen real model files it fails in all three ways a field can
fail.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Absent} from 7 of 15 --- including \code{qwen3},
        \code{gemma3}, \code{gemma-2-9b}, \code{Mistral-7B-v0.3} and
        \code{Llama-2}, all strongly multilingual.
  \item \textbf{Wrong} where present: \code{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} declares
        exactly \code{["en"]}; \code{Phi-4} declares \code{["en"]}.
  \item \textbf{Inverted by the transport.} Ollama's \code{server/routes.go}
        blanks any metadata array longer than five entries unless
        \code{verbose: true} is passed. Llama 3.1's real eight-language list
        --- \emph{containing} \code{es} --- therefore reads as \code{[]}, while
        Phi-4's misleading one-element \code{["en"]} survives intact.
\end{itemize}

The third failure is the decisive one, because it is not noise but \emph{sign
reversal}. A naive reader of this field concludes ``no languages declared'' for
the Spanish-capable model and ``English only'' for the model that is not.
Absence and \code{["en"]} are equally worthless as evidence against Spanish, and
a system that consumed this field would be confidently wrong in exactly the
direction that hurts. The implemented profile therefore reads \textbf{no
language metadata at all}.

\textbf{Tokenizer fertility is not a language signal --- a negative result.}

The attractive cheap measurement is the token tax: tokens spent on Spanish
relative to tokens spent on equivalent English. It is genuinely measurable ---
Ollama returns \code{prompt\_eval\_count} --- it requires no provider
cooperation, and it is genuinely useless for this decision. We tested it and
rejected it, and the paper records the rejection rather than the omission.

Measured on this project's own models (2026-07-28), Spanish-per-character
efficiency --- chars/token in Spanish over chars/token in English, same meaning,
baseline-subtracted --- is given in Table~\ref{tab:fertility-efficiency}.

Everything lies in $[0.74, 0.83]$. Any threshold that separates that band
mislabels \code{glm-5.2:cloud} --- a 756B frontier model with excellent Spanish,
and this operator's \emph{primary configured model} --- as English-biased. There
is no cut point that survives its own first user.

The literature explains why, and it does not contradict itself; it is a
statement about range. Fertility predicts accuracy where the range is
\textbf{huge}: across 10 LLMs and 16 African languages, regression slopes run
from $-0.08$ to $-0.18$ accuracy per additional token per word, explaining
20--50\% of variance (\code{arXiv:2509.05486}). It fails where the range is
\textbf{narrow}: a Ukrainian zero-shot study reports $\rho = -0.43$ at
$p = 0.34$ --- not significant (\code{arXiv:2605.14890}). Spanish is decisively
the narrow case: across 24 European languages and six tokenizers, English
averages 1.23 tokens per word and Spanish 1.46, an 8--29\% band
(\code{arXiv:2605.24718}). The metric is itself contested as a multilingual
evaluation instrument on independent grounds \citep{nayeem2025strr}.

Two further facts finish it, and they are not statistical but structural.

\textbf{The structural ceiling.} Every model in a family shares one tokenizer
and therefore one fertility number, while differing enormously in Spanish output
quality. Fertility cannot discriminate \emph{within} a family by construction
--- and within-family discrimination is most of what this profile is asked to
do.

\textbf{The counterexamples run backwards.} \code{Phi-4} has a
100{,}352-entry vocabulary and declares itself English-only;
\code{Mistral-7B-v0.3} has 32{,}768 and handles Spanish. The metric orders them
the wrong way round.

\begin{keyinsight}
\textbf{Observation 2.} Tokenizer fertility measures \textbf{cost}, not
competence. It remains useful for context budgeting and pricing. It does not
gate language routing.
\end{keyinsight}

One incidental result deserves a sentence and no more. Measuring an ``accent
surcharge'' --- the same words with diacritics added --- came out
\textbf{negative} for every real multilingual model: correctly-accented Spanish
tokenizes \emph{more cheaply}, because \code{cuánto} is one vocabulary entry
while the misspelled \code{cuanto} splits. It came out positive only for
\code{Orpheus-3b}, a 3B English fine-tune. That is suggestive of a byte-fallback
detector, and it is one data point against six. It is recorded, not relied upon.

\textbf{The resolution ladder.}

What remains is a measurement, arranged as three levels, cheapest first, none of
them on the request path.

\centering

\label{tab:resolution-ladder}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.16\textwidth}
                  >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.44\textwidth}
                  >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.24\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Level} & \textbf{What it is} & \textbf{Authority} \\
\midrule
\textbf{L0 \textperiodcentered{} SEED} &
  A name-shaped prior over the model id &
  \textbf{None.} Ordering only. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
\textbf{L1 \textperiodcentered{} PROBE} &
  Four graded tasks with known answers, run once per identity &
  \textbf{The authority.} \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
\textbf{L2 \textperiodcentered{} OBSERVE} &
  The same graders run free on real production responses &
  \textbf{Demotion only.} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\textbf{L0 --- the seed, and why it has no authority.} The seed exists for
exactly one purpose: to avoid assisting a model that is almost certainly fluent
during the seconds between binding it and its first probe returning. It is not
evidence. Because it is a regex over a model name --- the very artifact
Observation 1 forbids trusting --- it is constrained by three invariants rather
than described by a policy, and each is pinned by a test.

\begin{keyinsight}
\textbf{S1.} A probe verdict \textbf{always} overrides the seed.\\
\textbf{S2.} The seed may \textbf{never} emit \code{WEAK}. Demotion by regex is
the unrecoverable direction: a false \emph{``cannot do Spanish''} means the
model is never tried without assistance again, and the user sees silent
degradation with no error anywhere.\\
\textbf{S3.} Absence from the seed table means \code{UNKNOWN}, \textbf{never}
``no Spanish''.
\end{keyinsight}

S2 is the invariant that distinguishes this design from the hardcoded table it
replaces. A name-shaped prior is permitted to say \emph{``probably fine, don't
help yet''} and is forbidden from ever saying \emph{``this one is bad''}.

\textbf{L1 --- the probe.} Four tasks, weighted, with pure-Python graders:
language (weight 1.0 --- asked in Spanish, did it answer in Spanish or drift to
English), register (1.5 --- Angela's rule that verbs stay Spanish while American
technical nouns stay English, which grades \emph{instruction-following in
Spanish}, not vocabulary), semantic (2.0 --- Spanish intent to the correct tool
name, graded strictly: naming the right tool \textbf{and no other}), and charset
(1.0 --- do the diacritics survive the round trip, or return as mojibake).
Thresholds: $\geq 0.85$ \textrightarrow{} \code{FLUENT},
$\geq 0.45$ \textrightarrow{} \code{ASSIST}, otherwise \code{WEAK}.

Three properties of the probe matter more than its contents.

\emph{No model judges another model.} Every grader is a pure function of the
response string, so a verdict cannot drift between runs the way an
LLM-as-judge verdict does --- the same hazard \S8.5 gates the evaluation judge
against, avoided here by construction rather than by measurement.

\emph{The raw features are persisted, not just the verdict.} Per-check scores
and the model's own answers are stored alongside the tier, so thresholds can be
retuned \textbf{without re-probing anything}, and a human can audit or override
a verdict rather than merely observe it. A \code{PROBE\_VERSION} counter
invalidates verdicts whose features are no longer comparable; an older verdict
is treated as absent rather than trusted.

\emph{A transport failure is not evidence.} If more than one probe is
unreachable, the run returns \code{INCONCLUSIVE} and \textbf{is never cached}. A
dead connection says nothing about Spanish, and caching it would convert an
outage into a permanent verdict.

\textbf{L2 --- passive verification.} The same graders run free on real
production responses whenever the user's request was Spanish: zero tokens,
microseconds of CPU. Its authority is deliberately one-directional.

\begin{keyinsight}
\textbf{O1.} Observation may \textbf{demote} a standing verdict. It may
\textbf{never} promote one --- promotion requires a probe, because a run of easy
answers is not evidence.\\
\textbf{O2.} Demotion requires a \emph{sustained} failure rate (below 0.60) over
a \emph{real} sample (at least 12), within a bounded window (200, halved on
overflow) --- so neither does an old run of failures hold a model down forever,
nor does a good model bank credit against a later collapse.
\end{keyinsight}

The asymmetry between L1 and L2 also repairs a tension the earlier draft of this
section left open. Probes run at temperature 0 with a fixed seed where the
transport supports it, because a verdict that cannot be reproduced cannot be
audited or retuned --- but probing at temperature 0 systematically measures the
model's \textbf{best} case, which biases the profile optimistic, and optimism is
precisely the harmful direction. The resolution is not to abandon
reproducibility; it is to let the two levels compensate. L1 is reproducible and
optimistic, and is held to a deliberately high \code{FLUENT} threshold for that
reason. L2 runs at production temperature on production traffic, and carries the
demote authority. The one that can be trusted to be exact is not allowed to be
generous; the one that sees reality is not allowed to be flattering.

\textbf{Identity --- the cache key.}

A verdict is cached against an identity that changes if and only if the artifact
changes: the \textbf{GGUF blob sha256 digest} for a local model, which moves
when the weights or the tokenizer move and not otherwise; and the \textbf{exact
model id} for a cloud model, including the \code{:cloud} suffix and any size
tag.

Never the family name. An earlier revision collapsed \code{glm-5.2:cloud} and
\code{glm-5.2} to a single key, so a local artifact and a cloud endpoint that
merely share a family name would have shared one verdict --- a measurement of
one thing attributed to another. This is pinned by a test. It also subsumes part
of the freshness problem: a local model that is re-quantized or re-tokenized
gets a new identity automatically, and a cloud alias that is silently swapped
behind a stable id is caught not by a version string but by L2's demotion
authority, which does not care \emph{why} the answers got worse.

\textbf{Fail-open, and the direction of the asymmetry.}

Every failure path resolves to \code{ASSIST} --- the safe middle --- and never
to \code{FLUENT}. Nothing in the module raises into a caller and nothing blocks
a request. The direction is not a default; it is an argument.

A wrong \code{ASSIST} costs some hint tokens and slightly noisier scoring on a
request the model would have handled unaided, and it is corrected by the next
probe. A wrong \code{FLUENT} silently withholds the help a weak model needed,
produces \emph{``no tool matched''} on a request the system could have routed,
and reads to the operator as Tlamatini simply failing in Spanish --- with no
error, no log line and no way for the user to attribute the failure. The two
errors are not symmetric in cost, in detectability, or in recoverability, so the
fail-open direction is fixed by the one that is cheap and self-correcting rather
than by the one that is silent and durable.

\code{UNKNOWN} is therefore not a fourth behaviour. It is \code{ASSIST} under a
different name, and the name is kept only so that the operator-facing profile
can distinguish \emph{``measured as adequate''} from \emph{``never measured''}.

Folding (N1) is free and always on: it is the identity on ASCII, so it costs
nothing and is never gated. Expansion (N3) is what the tier actually decides.
Embedding models are excluded from the ladder entirely --- they never produce
prose, \code{/api/generate} rejects them outright, and probing them is
meaningless.

The module is \textbf{stdlib-only and never calls an LLM}: \code{run\_probe}
takes an \code{invoke(prompt) -> str} callable supplied by the caller. It is
therefore importable from a pool subprocess, testable against a fake, and
\emph{structurally} incapable of stalling the application --- which is the
mechanical form of \S6.11's rule that the profile is computed out of band, one
global worker, never on the request path, refusing to run while a generation is
in flight.

\textbf{Honest limits.}

Four, stated plainly, because a measurement section that reported only its
successes would be the thing this paper argues against.

\textbf{The thresholds are unvalidated.} $0.85$, $0.45$, $0.60$ and the sample
floor of $12$ encode the failure modes described above --- they do \textbf{not}
encode a measured correlation with MMLU-es or any other external Spanish
benchmark. No such validation has been performed. They are defensible choices,
not calibrated ones, and \S8's instrumentation is where they would first be
tested against outcomes.

\textbf{Four tasks is a smoke test, not a benchmark.} The battery detects gross
deficiency across four axes. It does not measure long-form, hard, or
domain-specific Spanish, and a model that passes it has demonstrated only that
it does not fail in the four ways that break this system first.

\textbf{The probe conflates instruction-following with language ability.} A
model that ignores \foreignlanguage{spanish}{\emph{``responde en español''}}
fails the language check, and it is arguable that what has been measured there
is compliance rather than competence. For this system's purposes the conflation
is tolerable --- an operator system needs a model that \emph{does what it is
told in Spanish}, not one that could in principle --- but it is a conflation,
and a verdict of \code{WEAK} should not be read as a claim about the model's
Spanish in general.

\textbf{The gate is implemented and tested, and not yet firing.}
\code{agent/i18n/model\_caps.py} and its 45 tests are complete, but every
production call site of \code{normalize\_request} --- four of them, across
\code{capability\_registry.py}, \code{global\_execution\_planner.py} and
\code{mcp\_agent.py} --- still invokes it with no model name, so today every
request takes the legacy path and the tier is never consulted. Threading the
model identity through the planner and the executor is a core change that
remains pending. Relatedly, the module emits a \textbf{tier and a Stage-2
policy}, not a ladder start rung: the tier-to-rung column in
Table~\ref{tab:profile-emits} is \emph{specified} here as the intended binding
of \S6.7's \code{profile.start\_rung}, and is not yet code. Nothing in \S6's
guarantee depends on this --- by Corollary 1 an unconsulted profile is simply a
profile that always starts at the conservative rung --- but the cost model of
\S6.11 is a projection until it does.

\subsection{The guarantee, restated for a hostile model}
\label{sec:guarantee-restated-hostile-model}

Take the worst admissible case: a model with no Spanish comprehension, no Spanish
generation, and a tendency to normalize literals. NEPANTLA's behaviour is then:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Execution: escalates to R3, which \emph{is} the English pipeline, with
        byte-exact literals. \textbf{$\theta \approx \theta_{EN}$.}
  \item Arguments: protected absolutely by V3. \textbf{Zero corruption.}
  \item Protocol: protected by V6; operator route pinned English for that model.
        \textbf{Zero silent routing damage.}
  \item Presentation: Spanish via the renderer, or Spanish-by-template as a floor.
        \textbf{The user never sees English.}
  \item Cost: 2--4 model calls instead of 1.
\end{itemize}

The user pays latency. They do not pay correctness, and they do not pay language.
